# System 1 v4: question origins and source terms

This inventory describes the 2,157-item v4 bank and its source declarations. It is not a final commercial-rights determination. The published v2 remains unchanged.

The 1,934 v2 questions are project benchmark development material. The available evidence links 1,134 question records exactly to the project's generated JEV decision bank, plus two revised descendants; 200 to the project's pilot (194 matching the captured pilot's question and options, six later revisions); six to a self-authored truth-teller supplement; and 592 to the logical and practical v2 development components. For the 592, the component lineage is documented but individual authors and possible external influences have not all been independently established. Pilot origin labels include user-supplied ChatGPT exports and other internal labels whose underlying authorship is not fully established. These categories describe evidence of development lineage, not a warranty that every underlying source right has been independently cleared. The existing System 1 repository publishes its project material under MIT; that project license does not change third-party rights.

The additions are borrowed, not project-invented exam questions:

* **200 AQuA-RAT quantitative questions.** The [upstream AQuA repository](https://github.com/google-deepmind/AQuA/tree/26b64ed1f22208c6742f47df547dfd65088ec30d) declares [Apache License 2.0](https://github.com/google-deepmind/AQuA/blob/26b64ed1f22208c6742f47df547dfd65088ec30d/LICENSE). The dataset describes these as crowdsourced algebraic word problems, not authenticated official GRE or SAT questions. The upstream license text, notice and attribution accompany the release. The benchmark retains the questions and options and adds wrapper text and metadata. Source commit, file, line and hash are recorded per item. Cite Ling et al., *Program Induction by Rationale Generation: Learning to Solve and Explain Algebraic Word Problems*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.04146.
* **23 MAIQ number-sequence questions.** The [IQTest repository](https://github.com/CognitiveAIGroup/IQTest) declares the [MIT license](https://github.com/CognitiveAIGroup/IQTest/blob/master/LICENSE). Its authors say the material was collected from IQ tests for humans. The original test and the underlying rights for each item have not been established. Repository-level licensing is not independent evidence that the repository holder controls every original question. The benchmark owner elected to include these 23 with this prominent caveat; they must not be described as confirmed commercially cleared. Each source item identifier and content hash is recorded. The retrieved `datas-0.2.6.0.tar.gz` archive has SHA-256 `654173a72140d5c5142c4071508278b926cc1b35f09d18ecdca6499f20c18fe6`; this pins its bytes, not a verified repository commit.

Two candidate questions from LogiQA 2.0, whose [upstream license is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://github.com/csitfun/LogiQA2.0), are kept in separate research and are not part of the v4 bank, in accordance with the user's decision. Five AQuA questions are separately held for quality issues and are not included in the bank.

Do not describe this mixed bank as entirely MIT or assert that every question is independently rights-cleared. Include machine-readable question-level provenance and the relevant full license notices with any eventual distribution. The source licenses and permissions must be assessed in context before commercial publication; provenance unknowns must remain visible rather than being filled by inference.

The original 60 quantitative questions were selected because Fastino and D3 previously answered them incorrectly. The 140 v4 additions were selected because at least one of Fastino, Mammoth, GPT-6 Sol without reasoning, D3 Ultra and Drex Max missed the source key. This distributes selection across models but does not eliminate selection bias. The 23 sequence items ask for a conventional best-supported pattern; finite sequences can have multiple mathematically possible continuations. These are admissions-style and IQ-style tasks, not human-normed scores for models or contamination-free material.
